## 1. Purpose

This document defines the research-level stability concepts to be incorporated into the SEXTANT PROTOCOL™ DP Resilience V&V architecture before construction of the MARIN V&V Scenario Test Matrix.

The purpose is to extend the existing environmental resilience assessment with a dedicated stability and controlled-recovery layer.

The stability layer is intended to examine:

- dynamic vessel stability;
- restoring behaviour;
- GZ-based restoring capability;
- righting moment;
- centre-of-gravity movement;
- centre-of-rotation behaviour;
- controlled rate of turn;
- controlled swing behaviour;
- transient environmental loading;
- recovery from disturbed conditions;
- interaction between primary and secondary resilience layers;
- stabilizer arbitration; and
- human-authorized simulated response.

This is a research and simulation framework only.

---

## 2. Core Stability Principle

The simulator shall not treat GM alone as the complete representation of vessel stability.

GM is an important initial-stability parameter, but the research model shall also consider the vessel's restoring behaviour through:

**GZ → Righting Arm → Righting Moment**

The fundamental relationship is represented conceptually as:

**Righting Moment = Δ × GZ**

where:

- Δ = vessel displacement;
- GZ = righting arm.

The simulator therefore evaluates stability as a dynamic and controlled recovery problem rather than as a single static GM threshold.

---

## 3. Dynamic Stability Concept

The research model shall distinguish between:

### Initial Stability

The vessel's response near the upright condition.

### Dynamic Stability

The vessel's ability to absorb and recover from changing disturbances over time.

### Restoring Capability

The available righting moment generated by the vessel's changing heel condition.

### Recovery Behaviour

The manner in which the vessel returns toward a controlled stable condition following a simulated disturbance.

### Controlled Stability

The ability to maintain or recover a safe simulated vessel state without generating excessive oscillation, uncontrolled swing or excessive rate of turn.

---

## 4. GZ and Righting Moment

The stability layer shall treat the GZ curve as a primary research input where appropriate.

Conceptually:

**GZ(heel angle) → Righting Moment(heel angle)**

The research simulator may evaluate:

- instantaneous GZ;
- maximum GZ;
- heel angle corresponding to maximum GZ;
- area under the GZ curve;
- available restoring moment;
- loss of restoring capability;
- progression toward an unstable condition.

The model shall not infer operational stability certification from these calculations.

---

## 5. Centre of Gravity

The research architecture shall account for changes in centre of gravity.

A simulated change in CG may result from:

- simulated cargo movement;
- simulated tank condition;
- simulated load transfer;
- simulated equipment movement;
- simulated ballast condition;
- simulated operational loading.

The effect of CG movement shall be evaluated in relation to:

**CG → GZ → Righting Moment → Dynamic Response**

A CG shift may therefore change the manner in which the simulated vessel responds to environmental loading.

---

## 6. Centre of Rotation

The research model shall distinguish the vessel's centre of gravity from the centre of rotation used in simulated manoeuvring behaviour.

The centre of rotation may influence:

- turning response;
- lateral acceleration;
- swing behaviour;
- heading response;
- position error;
- transient heel;
- interaction between environmental forces and simulated control response.

The simulator shall therefore treat controlled swing as a dynamic response rather than simply as a change in heading.

---

## 7. Rate of Turn

Rate of turn shall be treated as an important controlled-response variable.

The research model may evaluate:

**Rate of Turn → Swing Behaviour → Transient Loading → Stability Response**

An excessive simulated rate of turn may produce:

- increased transient loading;
- increased position error;
- increased heading deviation;
- increased lateral acceleration;
- increased simulated heel;
- reduced recovery margin.

The stabilizer shall therefore seek controlled rather than abrupt simulated responses.

---

## 8. Heeling-Pump Analogy

The SEXTANT stabilizer may be conceptually compared with a vessel's heeling-control function.

The analogy is:

**Primary Layer + Secondary Layer → Stabilizer → Controlled Correction**

Similar to a controlled heeling or ballast response, the stabilizer does not simply react to the magnitude of an event.

It considers:

- direction;
- magnitude;
- rate of change;
- persistence;
- restoring capability;
- available recovery margin;
- interaction between simultaneous disturbances.

This analogy is architectural and educational only.

It does not imply that the software controls physical heeling pumps.

## 9. Stability Modes

The research simulator shall support distinct stability modes.

### MODE 1 — STABLE

Normal restoring capability.

Expected response:

**MONITOR**

---

### MODE 2 — DYNAMICALLY STABLE

The vessel remains within the simulated recovery envelope but environmental conditions are changing.

Expected response:

**INCREASE MONITORING**

---

### MODE 3 — PRECAUTIONARY STABILITY

The available simulated recovery margin is reducing.

Expected response:

**PREPARE STABILISATION**

---

### MODE 4 — CRITICAL STABILITY

The simulated condition indicates significantly reduced recovery margin or severe environmental loading.

Expected response:

**SAFE STATE / HUMAN REVIEW**

---

### MODE 5 — PROTECTIVE SIMULATED STATE

The research model identifies a condition requiring maximum simulated protection.

Expected response:

**MAINTAIN SAFE STATE / ESCALATE**

No operational command is generated.

## 25. Heel and List — Combined Stability Model

The research simulator shall distinguish between heel and list while allowing both conditions to contribute to the overall simulated vessel attitude.

### Heel

Heel represents a dynamic or transient transverse inclination associated with changing forces and moments.

Potential contributors include:

- Wind loading;
- Wave loading;
- Current;
- Thrust;
- Turning;
- Rate of turn;
- Transient manoeuvring forces;
- Changing environmental conditions.

### List

List represents a persistent or quasi-static transverse inclination associated primarily with an asymmetric vessel condition.

Potential contributors include:

- Transverse CG shift;
- Uneven loading;
- Ballast condition;
- Tank condition;
- Cargo distribution;
- Simulated flooding or load asymmetry, where specifically modelled.

The simulator shall not assume that heel and list are the same physical condition.

The research model shall examine:

- Magnitude of heel;
- Rate of heel;
- Direction of heel;
- Duration;
- Rate of recovery;
- Available GZ;
- Righting moment;
- Environmental loading;
- Trim;
- CG position;
- Rate of turn;
- Recovery margin.

The objective is to distinguish:

**CONTROLLED DYNAMIC HEEL**

from

**PROGRESSIVE LOSS OF STABILITY**

and from

**PERSISTENT LIST CONDITION**.

## 29. Controlled Swing

Heel/list shall be evaluated together with controlled swing behaviour.

Conceptually:

**RATE OF TURN**
→ **LATERAL ACCELERATION**
→ **HEEL RESPONSE**
→ **GZ / RIGHTING RESPONSE**
→ **RECOVERY**

The stabilizer shall therefore evaluate not only the magnitude of vessel attitude but also the rate at which that attitude is changing.

A controlled response should avoid unnecessary abrupt corrections that could increase oscillation or transient loading.
