Background

3.1 Dynamic Positioning Environment

Dynamic Positioning (DP) is a marine vessel control technology used to maintain a vessel's position and heading through automated control of propulsion and thruster systems.

A DP operation typically depends upon the interaction of several functional elements, including:

- position reference systems;
- heading reference systems;
- environmental sensors;
- DP control computers;
- propulsion and thruster systems;
- power generation and distribution;
- vessel configuration;
- operator monitoring; and
- operational procedures.

The overall behaviour of a DP vessel is consequently influenced by both the vessel's technical systems and the external environment in which it operates.

The Sextant Protocol™ research simulator does not reproduce the complete engineering complexity of a certified DP system. Instead, it provides a controlled abstraction of selected environmental and resilience conditions for the purpose of investigating a separate research question: how a resilience-arbitration layer might interpret changing conditions and support human decision-making.

---

3.2 Environmental Loading

A DP vessel may experience environmental forces generated by:

- wind;
- current;
- waves;
- tidal conditions; and
- combinations of these factors.

The relative contribution of these conditions can change during an operation.

For research purposes, the Sextant Protocol™ simulator represents environmental conditions through controlled stress variables.

The current prototype includes:

Wind Stress — 0 to 100

Current Stress — 0 to 100

Wave Stress — 0 to 100

Tidal Stress — 0 to 100

These values are simulation parameters rather than direct measurements from a real vessel.

They allow repeatable scenarios to be generated and compared.

For example, a controlled increase in current stress can be used to investigate how the simulated resilience architecture responds when environmental loading increases.

---

3.3 Changing Conditions and Cascading Effects

A significant research consideration is that maritime events may not consist of a single isolated failure.

A developing situation can involve interacting conditions.

A simplified conceptual sequence may be represented as:

Environmental Change

↓

Increased Environmental Loading

↓

Increased Simulated System Demand

↓

Reduced Resilience Margin

↓

Increased Operator Attention Requirement

↓

Potential Escalation

This does not imply that every environmental increase will result in failure.

Rather, it illustrates why early recognition can be an important area of resilience research.

The Sextant Protocol™ Stabilizer is intended to investigate whether a deterministic arbitration layer can identify an elevated condition early enough to support structured operator preparation.

---

3.4 Resilience Versus Failure Detection

Traditional monitoring and alarm functions may identify a condition after a threshold has been reached.

The research proposition of Sextant Protocol™ is different.

The simulator investigates whether resilience assessment can operate before a defined failure state occurs.

The conceptual distinction is:

Failure Detection

«Identify that a defined condition has occurred.»

versus:

Resilience Assessment

«Identify that conditions are changing in a direction that may reduce operational resilience and provide structured guidance before escalation.»

The simulator does not claim that this distinction represents a replacement for existing DP alarms, fault detection or protection systems.

Instead, it provides a research environment for examining whether the two concepts can complement one another.

---

3.5 Decision Complexity

A DP operator may need to consider multiple information sources simultaneously.

These may include:

- vessel position;
- heading;
- environmental conditions;
- thruster demand;
- power availability;
- sensor integrity;
- alarms;
- equipment status;
- operational requirements; and
- developing trends.

The challenge is therefore not necessarily a lack of information.

In some situations, the challenge may be the interpretation and prioritisation of multiple changing signals.

The Sextant Protocol™ research architecture addresses this problem through layered assessment and deterministic arbitration.

The objective is to transform multiple simulated inputs into a structured resilience state that can be presented to the human operator.

---

3.6 Independent Assessment

The proposed architecture uses two assessment pathways.

Primary AI

The Primary AI provides the principal simulated assessment.

It evaluates the environmental and system state according to the research rules implemented in the simulator.

Secondary AI

The Secondary AI provides an independent safety-oriented assessment.

The purpose of this second pathway is to create an additional perspective before the final resilience state is determined.

The two assessments are then presented to the Stabilizer.

This architecture creates an important research property:

The final resilience state is not necessarily determined by a single assessment pathway.

Instead:

S1 + S2 → Stabilizer Arbitration

This allows disagreement, agreement, or differing levels of concern to become subjects of simulation and analysis.

---

3.7 The Stabilizer Concept

The Stabilizer is the central research component of the Sextant Protocol™ architecture.

Its function is resilience arbitration.

The Stabilizer receives the available assessment outputs and determines the appropriate simulated resilience state according to deterministic rules.

The conceptual function is:

INPUTS

Environmental state
+
Primary assessment
+
Secondary assessment
+
System state

↓

STABILIZER

Deterministic arbitration

↓

OUTPUT

Resilience state
+
Risk classification
+
Operator attention
+
Decision-support recommendation

The Stabilizer does not directly control a real vessel.

Its research purpose is to investigate whether a controlled arbitration layer can prevent the decision process from becoming fragmented when environmental conditions and assessment outputs change.

---

3.8 Preventive Arbitration

The current simulator includes a PREVENTIVE ARBITRATION state.

This state represents a condition in which the system has identified an elevated simulated condition but has not reached a defined requirement for emergency escalation.

For example, the CURRENT_SURGE scenario produced:

- Environmental Stress: 62.25
- Risk: MEDIUM
- Primary AI: ELEVATED CONTROL
- Secondary AI: INDEPENDENT SAFETY ADVISORY
- Stabilizer: PREVENTIVE ARBITRATION
- Resilience State: PREVENTIVE MONITORING

The resulting recommendation was:

MAINTAIN DP WITH INCREASED OPERATOR ATTENTION

This is an important characteristic of the research concept.

The objective is not to force every elevated condition into an emergency response.

Instead, the architecture attempts to provide a graduated response proportional to the simulated condition.

---

3.9 Human Decision-Support

The Stabilizer's output is converted into information that can be considered by the operator.

The current simulator provides recommendations such as:

- maintain simulated DP operations;
- increase monitoring;
- review simulated thruster and power demand;
- verify sensor consistency;
- verify environmental inputs; and
- prepare contingency procedures if conditions deteriorate.

These are simulation recommendations only.

They are not operational instructions and must not be interpreted as substitutes for vessel-specific procedures, DP manuals, emergency procedures, or professional judgment.

The research objective is to examine whether structured recommendations can make a developing condition easier to interpret and respond to within the simulated environment.

---

3.10 Human Authority

Human authority remains central to the current architecture.

The simulator explicitly records:

Human Authority: FINAL

and:

Autonomous Command: FALSE

Operational Authority: FALSE

Real Vessel Connection: FALSE

This provides a clear separation between:

Assessment

and

Authority.

The simulator can assess a condition and recommend an action.

The human operator retains the final decision.

---

3.11 Research Gap

The Sextant Protocol™ research programme is motivated by an area requiring further investigation:

How can deterministic resilience assessment and independent analytical layers be used to provide structured decision-support before a simulated maritime event becomes a cascading condition?

The research simulator provides a controlled environment in which this question can be examined.

It allows environmental conditions to be:

- generated;
- repeated;
- intensified;
- compared;
- logged;
- audited; and
- independently reviewed.

This repeatability is important because a resilience architecture should not be evaluated solely through individual demonstrations.

It should be subjected to systematic testing across controlled scenarios.

---

3.12 Research Position

The Sextant Protocol™ is therefore positioned as a research and simulation framework rather than an alternative DP control system.

Its principal research contribution is the combination of:

Controlled Environmental Simulation

+ 

Independent Assessment

+ 

Deterministic Resilience Arbitration

+ 

Human-in-the-Loop Decision Support

+ 

Repeatable Auditability

The purpose of this combination is to investigate whether an additional resilience layer can provide useful early recognition and structured decision-support while preserving human authority.
