Executive Summary

Sextant Protocol™ – DP Resilience Research Simulator

A Deterministic Layered Architecture for Early Recognition, Resilience Arbitration and Human-in-the-Loop Decision Support

Dynamic Positioning (DP) operations take place in environments where wind, waves, currents, tides, vessel condition, sensor integrity and system demand can change rapidly. These changing conditions can increase operational complexity and require timely recognition, assessment and response.

The Sextant Protocol™ DP Resilience Research Simulator is a deterministic research and engineering simulation environment developed to investigate whether an additional resilience architecture can provide earlier recognition of changing conditions, structured escalation and improved decision-support within a controlled and repeatable virtual maritime environment.

The research architecture is represented as:

ENVIRONMENT → PRIMARY AI → SECONDARY AI → STABILIZER → HUMAN AUTHORITY → SIMULATED DP RESPONSE

The simulator generates controlled environmental scenarios involving wind, current, wave and tidal loading. These conditions are processed through independent analytical pathways before being evaluated by a deterministic Stabilizer.

The Stabilizer is the central resilience-arbitration component of the research architecture.

Its purpose is not to replace the DP system or the human operator. Instead, it is designed to examine whether independent assessments can be combined into a structured resilience state before deteriorating conditions develop into a cascading simulated event.

The resulting assessment can generate:

- resilience status;
- risk classification;
- operator attention level;
- early-warning notification;
- recommended response mode;
- structured operator actions; and
- a simulated DP response.

The present prototype deliberately maintains human authority as final. No autonomous operational command is issued, and the simulator has no connection to an operational vessel.

Demonstration Scenario

A representative CURRENT_SURGE scenario was executed within the research simulator.

The recorded simulation produced:

- Environmental Stress: 62.25
- Risk Classification: MEDIUM
- Primary AI: ELEVATED CONTROL
- Secondary AI: INDEPENDENT SAFETY ADVISORY
- Stabilizer: PREVENTIVE ARBITRATION
- Human Authority: FINAL
- Autonomous Command: FALSE
- Operational Authority: FALSE
- Real Vessel Connection: FALSE

The Stabilizer generated an ELEVATED MONITORING state rather than allowing the simulated condition to progress directly toward a higher escalation state.

The resulting operator recommendation was:

MAINTAIN DP WITH INCREASED OPERATOR ATTENTION

with the following simulated decision-support actions:

1. Maintain simulated DP operations.
2. Increase operator monitoring of environmental trends.
3. Review simulated thruster and power demand.
4. Verify sensor consistency and environmental inputs.
5. Prepare contingency procedures if conditions continue to deteriorate.

This demonstration illustrates the principal research concept: the Stabilizer operates as a resilience-arbitration layer, helping transform changing environmental conditions and independent assessments into structured operator guidance before escalation.

Research Hypothesis

The central research question is:

«Can a layered deterministic resilience architecture provide earlier recognition, structured escalation and improved decision-support during rapidly changing simulated maritime conditions?»

The current simulator does not claim to prove this hypothesis in real-world vessel operations.

Instead, it provides a controlled environment in which the hypothesis can be:

- demonstrated;
- challenged;
- measured;
- repeated;
- independently reviewed; and
- progressively validated.

Human Authority

The current architecture is explicitly human-in-the-loop.

The simulator provides assessment and decision-support but does not replace:

- the Master;
- Dynamic Positioning Operator;
- authorised vessel operator;
- vessel procedures;
- DP control systems;
- safety management systems; or
- classification and regulatory requirements.

The final decision remains with the authorised human operator.

Operational Separation

The prototype is intentionally separated from operational vessel systems.

It does not connect to:

- Dynamic Positioning control systems;
- propulsion systems;
- steering systems;
- navigation systems;
- safety systems;
- vessel automation systems; or
- operational vessel networks.

All environmental conditions, sensor states, vessel behaviour, AI assessments, Stabilizer decisions and simulated responses are representations generated within the research environment.

Future Research

The Sextant Protocol™ research programme is intended to progress through increasingly rigorous validation stages, including:

1. deterministic software testing;
2. repeatable scenario benchmarking;
3. failure and cascade simulations;
4. human factors and operator decision-support studies;
5. software-in-the-loop testing;
6. hardware-in-the-loop simulation;
7. independent engineering review;
8. controlled simulator trials; and
9. evaluation against applicable maritime standards and classification requirements.

Only after appropriate independent validation could any consideration be given to future integration with operational systems.

Conclusion

The Sextant Protocol™ DP Resilience Research Simulator proposes a layered approach in which environmental conditions are assessed independently, resilience assessments are deterministically arbitrated, and structured guidance is presented to a human operator.

The central concept is not autonomous control.

It is resilience of the decision process.

The Stabilizer is intended to provide a controlled point of arbitration between environmental stress, independent assessments and human decision-making, with the research objective of recognising developing conditions early enough to support informed intervention before a simulated cascade develops.

This white paper presents the architecture as a research proposition for technical examination, independent validation and further development within the maritime and offshore engineering communities.
