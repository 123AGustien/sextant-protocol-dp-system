DP Resilience Research Cockpit

7.1 Purpose

The Sextant Protocol™ DP Resilience Cockpit provides the visual research interface through which the deterministic resilience architecture can be observed, exercised and evaluated.

The cockpit presents environmental conditions, simulated vessel status, analytical assessments, Stabilizer arbitration, operator guidance and audit information within a single research environment.

Its purpose is to make the resilience process visible and traceable.

The cockpit is not intended to reproduce the full human-machine interface of a certified Dynamic Positioning system.

It is a research instrument for investigating resilience behaviour.

7.18 Demonstration Significance

The demonstration does not establish that the Sextant Protocol™ architecture improves operational DP safety.

It does demonstrate that the prototype can represent a layered resilience pathway in a controlled environment.

Specifically, the demonstration shows a sequence in which:

1. an environmental scenario is selected;
2. environmental stress is calculated;
3. independent analytical states are generated;
4. the Stabilizer arbitrates the assessments;
5. a resilience state is established;
6. operator attention is increased;
7. structured recommendations are presented;
8. human authority remains final; and
9. no operational command is issued.

This is the mechanism that future experiments can measure.

---

7.19 Research Evidence Versus Operational Evidence

The distinction between demonstration evidence and operational evidence is critical.

The current cockpit provides:

Prototype Evidence

It does not yet provide:

Operational Safety Evidence

Operational claims would require substantially more validation, including appropriate engineering analysis, representative system modelling, independent verification and validation, human-factors evaluation, cybersecurity assessment, fault injection, hardware-in-the-loop testing and relevant regulatory or classification review.

The white paper therefore deliberately avoids presenting the current prototype as certified or operationally validated.

---

7.20 Reproducibility

The deterministic nature of the simulator provides an opportunity for reproducible experiments.

A research scenario can be defined by:

- environmental inputs;
- vessel parameters;
- sensor state;
- scenario type;
- AI assessment rules;
- Stabilizer rules; and
- initial system state.

The same configuration can then be executed repeatedly.

Researchers can compare:

RUN 1

against:

RUN 2

against:

RUN N

and determine whether the system produces consistent results.

This is an essential step toward formal validation.
