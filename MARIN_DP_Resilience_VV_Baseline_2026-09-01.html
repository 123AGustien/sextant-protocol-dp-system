SEXTANT PROTOCOL™ DP RESILIENCE RESEARCH SIMULATOR MARIN V&V BASELINE SPECIFICATION Document ID: SP-DP-MARIN-VV-001 Version: 1.0 Status: Pre-Development Baseline Date: 1 September 2026 Research Programme: MARIN USV / DP Resilience V&V Research Software Baseline: "marin-vv-baseline-2026-09-01" Baseline Commit: "a8b46ce" --- 1. PURPOSE This document establishes the preserved engineering baseline for the Sextant Protocol™ DP Resilience Research Simulator before further MARIN-oriented verification and validation development. The purpose of the baseline is to provide a fixed and reproducible reference against which future engineering modifications, experiments, validation activities and research results may be compared. The baseline is intentionally preserved before further development. Principle: «PRESERVE BASELINE → DEVELOP → TEST → CHALLENGE → MEASURE → REPEAT» --- 2. BASELINE STATUS The current software is classified as: RESEARCH / SIMULATION PROTOTYPE The baseline: - has no operational vessel connection; - has no physical vessel interface; - does not transmit DP commands; - does not control propulsion; - does not control navigation; - does not control steering; - does not control safety systems; - is not certified marine control software; and - provides research-oriented decision support and simulated responses only. Operational authority: NONE Autonomous command authority: FALSE Human authority: FINAL --- 3. BASELINE SOFTWARE The preserved baseline is represented by: Repository: Sextant Protocol™ DP Resilience System Branch / Release: "marin-vv-baseline-2026-09-01" Commit: "a8b46ce" The baseline preserves the complete engineering state immediately before further MARIN-oriented V&V development. No experimental modification should be made directly to the preserved baseline. --- 4. RESEARCH ARCHITECTURE The baseline architecture is: ENVIRONMENT ↓ PRIMARY AI ↓ SECONDARY AI ↓ STABILIZER ↓ RECOMMENDATION ↓ CAPTAIN AI LENA ↓ HUMAN AUTHORITY ↓ SIMULATED DP RESPONSE The architecture investigates whether layered resilience assessment and deterministic arbitration can provide structured recognition, escalation and decision support when simulated maritime conditions deteriorate. This remains a research hypothesis. No performance or safety conclusion is claimed by the baseline itself. --- 5. ENVIRONMENT MODEL The baseline provides four environmental stress inputs: Wind Range: "0–100" Current Range: "0–100" Wave Range: "0–100" Tidal Range: "0–100" The current deterministic weighting is: Environmental factor| Weight Wind| 30% Current| 30% Wave| 25% Tidal| 15% Total| 100% Environmental stress is calculated deterministically from these inputs. --- 6. RISK CLASSIFICATION The baseline classifies environmental stress as: Stress| Classification "< 30"| LOW "30–49.99"| MEDIUM "≥ 50"| HIGH These thresholds are baseline parameters. They are not presented as certified maritime limits. A future V&V programme may challenge, modify, justify or replace these parameters based on evidence. --- 7. PRIMARY AI The Primary AI provides the first simulated assessment response. The baseline supports four response states: - NORMAL CONTROL - ADAPTIVE CONTROL - ENHANCED CONTROL - CRITICAL CONTROL The Primary AI does not issue an operational vessel command. Its output is a research-simulation assessment. --- 8. SECONDARY AI The Secondary AI provides an independent safety-oriented assessment layer. Baseline states include: - SAFETY MONITOR - PRECAUTIONARY - PROTECTIVE - CRITICAL SAFETY The Secondary AI contributes to the subsequent stabilizer arbitration. --- 9. STABILIZER The Stabilizer is a central research component. Its purpose is to investigate deterministic arbitration between assessment layers. Baseline states include: - STABLE - BALANCED - PROTECTIVE - CRITICAL ARBITRATION The Stabilizer does not independently control a vessel. Its research purpose is to: 1. identify developing conditions; 2. identify conflicting assessment states; 3. structure escalation; 4. classify the simulated resilience condition; 5. provide structured decision support; and 6. preserve human decision authority. --- 10. CAPTAIN AI LENA Captain AI Lena operates in the baseline as a decision-support layer. Lena receives the simulated assessment and produces: - recommendation; - urgency; - response mode; - proposed simulated response; and - explanatory decision-support messaging. Baseline autonomous command state: FALSE Lena does not possess independent operational authority. --- 11. HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP The baseline contains an explicit human decision gate. The available simulated human decisions are: ACKNOWLEDGE CONDITION Records awareness of the simulated condition without authorising a response. MAINTAIN SAFE STATE Records a human decision to maintain the simulated safe state. AUTHORIZE SIMULATED RESPONSE Authorises execution of the simulated response inside the research environment only. The human operator remains the final authority. --- 12. SIMULATED RESPONSE The response mechanism is intentionally isolated from real vessel systems. When a simulated response is authorised, the software records: EXECUTION: SIMULATED ONLY OPERATIONAL DP COMMAND: NONE AUTONOMOUS COMMAND: FALSE The baseline therefore does not represent an operational DP controller. --- 13. SIMULATED VESSEL The baseline provides a virtual vessel profile: Name: SEXTANT-MPSV-01 Type: Multi-Purpose Support Vessel DP Class: DP2-SIMULATED Length: 85 m Beam: 20 m Draft: 6 m Main Thrusters: 2 Tunnel Thrusters: 2 Total Thrusters: 4 Nominal Simulated Thrust: 100 units These values define the research simulation environment. They do not represent a certified vessel model or a real vessel configuration. --- 14. BASELINE SCENARIOS The simulator provides repeatable scenario presets including: - NORMAL - MODERATE - HEAVY WEATHER - CRITICAL - CURRENT SURGE - HEAVY SEA - WIND GUST - COMBINED - RANDOM These scenarios provide initial repeatability for future testing. --- 15. AUDITABILITY The baseline produces a simulation audit containing events associated with: - simulator initialization; - environmental assessment; - Primary AI assessment; - Secondary AI assessment; - Stabilizer arbitration; - Lena decision support; - human authorization gate; and - simulated response status. The objective is to establish a traceable chain from environmental input to final simulated outcome. --- 16. BASELINE LIMITATIONS The following limitations are explicitly acknowledged. 16.1 Environmental abstraction The environmental model uses simplified scalar stress inputs. It is not a high-fidelity hydrodynamic or vessel-motion model. 16.2 Simplified vessel dynamics The baseline does not constitute a validated mathematical model of a real DP vessel. 16.3 Simulated response The baseline response is representational and does not model all physical consequences of a real manoeuvre. 16.4 AI terminology The Primary AI, Secondary AI and Captain AI Lena terminology describes architectural research layers. The baseline does not establish that these components constitute certified autonomous-control AI. 16.5 Certification No classification, statutory or regulatory certification is claimed. --- 17. PROPOSED MARIN V&V DEVELOPMENT The preserved baseline provides the starting point for a future V&V programme. The proposed progression is: BASELINE ↓ SOFTWARE TESTING ↓ REPEATABLE SCENARIOS ↓ FAULT INJECTION ↓ BASELINE COMPARISON ↓ HUMAN-FACTORS TESTING ↓ HIGH-FIDELITY SIMULATION ↓ HARDWARE-IN-THE-LOOP ↓ INDEPENDENT VERIFICATION & VALIDATION ↓ TECHNICAL / CLASSIFICATION REVIEW Each stage should be evidence-driven. No later stage should be interpreted as automatically validating an earlier research hypothesis. --- 18. PROPOSED V&V QUESTIONS The future research programme may investigate: 1. Does the layered architecture detect environmental deterioration earlier than a defined baseline? 2. Does deterministic arbitration reduce conflicting assessment states? 3. Does the architecture provide more structured escalation? 4. Are recommendations repeatable under identical inputs? 5. Are results deterministic and auditable? 6. How does fault injection affect each architectural layer? 7. How does the architecture behave under simultaneous disturbances? 8. How does human decision-making interact with the recommendation layer? 9. What failure modes are introduced by the architecture? 10. What failure modes remain undetected? 11. Which results can be independently reproduced? 12. Which claims cannot yet be supported by evidence? Negative results are considered valid research results. --- 19. INDEPENDENT REVIEW The project welcomes independent technical challenge. MARIN or other qualified research and engineering organisations may independently assess: - architecture; - assumptions; - environmental model; - thresholds; - deterministic calculations; - scenario definitions; - fault-injection methodology; - human-in-the-loop design; - auditability; - limitations; and - proposed validation methodology. The objective is not to demonstrate success prematurely. The objective is to determine whether the resilience proposition survives independent testing. --- 20. CHANGE CONTROL The preserved baseline shall remain unchanged. Future development should occur from a separate development branch. Every material modification should be: 1. identified; 2. version controlled; 3. tested; 4. documented; 5. compared against the baseline; and 6. associated with an auditable result. Where practical, experimental results should identify: - software version; - commit; - scenario; - input parameters; - expected result; - actual result; - pass/fail status; - observations; and - limitations. --- 21.
